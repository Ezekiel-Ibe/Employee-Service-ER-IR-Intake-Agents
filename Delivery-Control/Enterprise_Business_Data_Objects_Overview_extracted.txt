1. Executive summary
The enterprise business data model provides a system agnostic view of the information needed to represent the organisation. It groups information into stable business objects such as Party, Customer, Supplier, Product, Worker, Asset, Order, Invoice, Payment and Journal Entry. Each object has its own identity, lifecycle and relationships to other objects.
Core design idea
Shared objects are defined once then reused across business processes. Customer, Supplier and Worker are roles linked to Party. Product, Location, Organisation, Currency, Calendar and Status provide common context. Transactions record events such as orders, invoices, payments, stock movements, work, payroll and accounting postings.


The current active scope is organised into six vertical slices: Order to Cash, Procure to Pay, Inventory and Logistics, Finance and Performance, Asset and Maintenance, plus Hire to Retire. Shared reference data connects these slices. The model uses informational foreign keys, which means relationships must be validated through loading and quality controls rather than assumed to be enforced by the platform.

3. Shared enterprise spine
The shared enterprise spine gives all six vertical slices a common language. These objects prevent each process from creating its own incompatible version of the same concept.
Object
Business purpose
How it connects

Party
A person or organisation participating in a business relationship
Customer, Supplier and Worker roles link to Party.

Organisation
A legal entity or business unit within the enterprise group
Owns or contextualises accounts, payroll, locations, budgets and forecasts.

Location
A physical or logical place where business activities occur
Used by inventory, shipments, assets and work orders.

Product
Something offered sold purchased stocked manufactured maintained or retired
Referenced by orders, inventory, shipments, assets and quality events.

Currency and Exchange Rate
Monetary denomination plus conversion between currencies.
Used across orders, invoices, payments, budgets, forecasts and payroll.

Business Calendar
A date and period reference for financial and operational reporting.
Supports due dates, accounting periods, payroll periods and operational working days.

Status
A governed state value for a business object.
Provides consistent lifecycle states across master and transaction objects.

11. Governance and traceability
Control object
Purpose

Policy
Defines the governing rule or requirement.

Approval
Records a decision against a specific business object.

Audit Event
Records a change or access event for traceability.

Source identifiers
Preserve the system and source record from which a Fabric record was obtained.

Effective dating
Supports historical versions of master and reference objects.

Data classification and quality status
Provide governance state for publication and use.


Relationship enforcement
Foreign keys are informational in the active model. Pipelines and data quality checks must therefore validate that parent objects exist, dates are valid and financial or quantity reconciliations balance.


12. How developers should navigate the model
Step
Action
Developer interpretation

1
Start with the business object
Identify the object needed by the process, for example Customer, Product, Invoice or Worker.

2
Follow the canonical identifier
Use the object identifier to connect transactions and master data.

3
Use shared reference objects
Resolve currency, status, calendar, country and unit values through governed references.

4
Respect the grain
Keep headers, lines, events, balances and snapshots separate.

5
Preserve source traceability
Retain the source system, source record identifier and ingestion metadata.

6
Validate relationships
Apply referential, temporal and reconciliation controls during loading.

7
Publish through Gold and Magenta
Use Gold for analytical consumption. Use Magenta for approved terms, measures and ontology relationships.

14. Scope and current governance status
The model configuration is active at version 1.0.0. The business glossary entries remain proposed with owners and stewards recorded as TBD. This document therefore describes the intended high level business model. It does not replace field-level design approval or physical schema validation.
